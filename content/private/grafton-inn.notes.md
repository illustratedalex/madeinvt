# Research

- The Grafton Inn has operated continuously since 1801, making it one of the oldest continuously operating inns in Vermont. Confirm this claim with the Windham Foundation before using it in public content.
- The Windham Foundation is the nonprofit that owns and maintains the village of Grafton, the inn, and several other properties. Understanding this context is essential — Grafton is not a typical commercial tourist town, it's a foundation-preserved village.
- Grafton Ponds Outdoor Center (adjacent to the inn) operates cross-country skiing, mountain biking, and disc golf. It is a separate entity from the inn but typically booked as a package. Confirm current relationship and joint offerings.
- The Grafton Village Cheese Company is in Grafton (different ownership from the Windham Foundation). Confirm current status — it may be relevant as a nearby food/shopping option.

# Seasonal Notes

- **Fall (September–October):** Peak demand. Rooms book months ahead. Foliage arrives in the hills surrounding the village slightly later than lower elevations — check annual color reports.
- **Winter (December–March):** Cross-country skiing from the adjacent Grafton Ponds. Cozy tavern atmosphere. Lower room rates than fall.
- **Summer:** Gardens, pool, village walks. Less dramatic than fall but genuinely lovely. Good for families.
- **Spring:** Mud season reduces appeal. Limited programming. Probably lowest-traffic season.

# Local Tips

- The Old Tavern bar is reportedly excellent and worth visiting even for non-guests — worth verifying and noting in public content eventually.
- Grafton is approximately 45 minutes from Brattleboro — makes it feasible as a day trip from the I-91 corridor even without overnight.
- The village road through Grafton is one of the most photographed streets in Vermont. Weekday mornings in fall are the best window to shoot without cars.
