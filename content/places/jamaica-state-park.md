# Story

Jamaica State Park sits along the West River in one of the quieter corners of Windham County, a place where the campground fills early on summer weekends not because of spectacle but because of ease. The river runs alongside the main camping area, accessible enough for kids and calm enough for the kind of afternoon that has no agenda.

The park traces an old railroad grade that once ran freight through the valley, and the trail system follows that history: wide, level, and navigable without a map. In spring, the West River swells and the annual whitewater race draws paddlers from across New England, transforming the park into a temporary festival. By midsummer the same stretch becomes a swimming corridor, shallow enough for families and cool enough to matter on a hot Vermont afternoon.

Fall changes the character entirely. The campground thins out, the leaf cover turns the rail trail into something resembling a tunnel of color, and the park becomes a place for longer, quieter walks rather than afternoon splashing. Winter closes camping but keeps the trails open, and the wide flat grade offers one of the more approachable snowshoeing routes in the county for visitors who didn't come prepared for a backcountry outing.


# Why Visit

- **Three distinct seasons in one place.** Swimming river access in summer, exceptional rail trail foliage in fall, and snowshoeing-friendly terrain in winter make this a park worth returning to across the year.
- **Family baseline, no car required once parked.** Once you're in the campground, the river, trails, picnic areas, and ball fields are all on foot. Good for families who want an unhurried two days without logistics.
- **Gateway to Hamilton Falls.** The park sits two miles from the Hamilton Falls trailhead, making it the natural base camp for a Windham County waterfall day.


# Visitor Tips

- **Parking:** Day-use parking lot near the entrance. State park day-use fee applies. Camping registration required for overnight.
- **Restrooms:** Flush toilets and shower facilities available at the campground. Seasonal pit toilets at day-use area.
- **Dogs:** Allowed on leash in most areas. Not permitted in the swimming area during designated swim hours.
- **Difficulty:** Easy to moderate. The rail trail is nearly flat. Side trails toward the gorge involve some elevation change.
- **Visit length:** Half day for day trippers; 1–3 nights for campers.
- **Camping:** 18 tent sites, 43 lean-to sites, and a group camping area. Reservations open in early spring and peak weekends sell out within days. Book early.
- **Whitewater season:** The spring ball drop (usually early April) draws crowds and closes some areas temporarily. Plan accordingly or embrace the event.
- **Swim area:** Staffed swimming area on the West River, seasonal. Check Vermont State Parks for lifeguard schedule.


# Photography Tips

- **Best angle:** The covered bridge near the entrance is the most photogenic single frame in the park. Shoot at golden hour for warm light on the wood siding.
- **Light window:** Morning fog over the river in late summer and early fall is exceptional. Arrive before 7 a.m. to catch it.
- **Seasonal notes:**
  - *Spring:* High water and whitewater energy. Best for action photography on the river.
  - *Summer:* Wide shots of the swimming area with Vermont hills as backdrop. River reflections in early morning.
  - *Fall:* Rail trail tunnel shots looking east with backlit foliage. Late September through mid-October.
  - *Winter:* Snow-covered lean-tos and the frozen river edge. Quiet and underused as a photo subject.
- **Drone footage:** Open enough for drone work in the main campground area. Avoid flying over the swim area when staffed. Check Vermont State Parks drone policy before flying.


# Parking

Main entrance on Salmon Hole Lane off Route 30, Jamaica, VT 05343. Day-use fee collected at entrance booth (seasonal hours). Overflow parking available along the entrance road on busy summer weekends.


# Accessibility

The main rail trail is accessible for wheelchairs and mobility aids — wide, packed gravel, nearly flat for the first mile. The campground loop road is paved. Some tent sites are on uneven ground; lean-to sites are generally more accessible. Contact Vermont State Parks directly to request accessible site assignment.

# Best Time to Visit

**Spring (April–May):** Whitewater season, high river, wildflowers on the rail trail. Campground opens mid-May.

**Summer (June–August):** Peak season. Swimming, camping, and Hamilton Falls access. Book campsites well in advance.

**Fall (September–October):** Best foliage on the rail trail. Campground less crowded after Labor Day. Day-use remains excellent.

**Winter (November–March):** Campground closed. Trails open for snowshoeing and cross-country skiing. No facilities.
